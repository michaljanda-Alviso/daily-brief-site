Starting with health and peptide research. Out of the STAT Summit in Boston, Merck and Moderna's chief executives sat down together and offered a candid look at where their personalized messenger R N A cancer vaccine program stands. A schoolteacher treated for melanoma shared her experience publicly this week, putting a human face on what has been an encouraging clinical story. The approach pairs Merck's immunotherapy drug Keytruda with a custom-built messenger R N A vaccine that is designed specifically around the genetic mutations in an individual patient's tumor. The early results are promising enough that both companies say there is, in their words, a lot of exploring still to do. What matters practically is the word personalized. This is not a one-size vaccine. It is a sequence built from a biopsy of your specific cancer. The distance from research lab to widespread clinical availability is still meaningful, but the data is trending in the right direction and the patient outcomes being reported are generating real momentum. Separately, Verastem Oncology reported early encouraging results for its K R A S G twelve D inhibitor in pancreatic cancer patients, another cancer type with historically poor treatment options. Both stories point to the same direction: precision targeting at the molecular level, using the body's own biology as the map.

Now for your artificial intelligence move today. The new ChatGPT Chrome extension tab-reading feature is live, and the most useful thing you can do with it this morning is build a research workflow you have not been able to build before. Here is the specific experiment. Open five or six browser tabs that represent a decision you need to make today. Maybe it is tabs from competitor websites, a market report, a job posting you are considering, an industry article. Then, in the ChatGPT desktop or web app, tag Chrome and ask it to synthesize what it sees across those tabs into a single briefing. Ask it to surface contradictions, gaps, or the single most important thing you are missing. What you are testing is not whether the tool is clever. You are testing whether ambient tab awareness changes how quickly you move from raw information to a decision. Run it once this morning and note how long it takes versus your usual method. That one data point will tell you immediately whether this belongs in your daily workflow.
