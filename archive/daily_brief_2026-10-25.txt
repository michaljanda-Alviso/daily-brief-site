Let us start with technology and artificial intelligence, because there is a story worth sitting with this morning. An economist at Apollo Global Management is pushing back hard against the prevailing narrative around artificial intelligence investment. Despite massive capital flowing into the sector, the technology has delivered, in his words, zero financial payoff to corporate America outside of the tech industry itself. That is a striking claim, and it lands at a moment when enterprise software companies, manufacturers, and financial firms have all been racing to announce their artificial intelligence strategies. The Apollo economist's argument is essentially this: the productivity gains that artificial intelligence is supposed to unlock have not shown up in earnings or output data for most companies. The tech industry benefits because it sells the infrastructure and the tools. Everyone else is still waiting. That distinction matters if you are thinking about where real value is accumulating right now.

On a lighter note from the artificial intelligence world, Grok, the artificial intelligence system backed by Elon Musk's xAI, has released the first seven minutes of what it is calling Odyssey, a fully artificial-intelligence-generated film. The early reaction is measured. Critics and viewers alike describe the footage as technically coherent but artistically hollow. The images move, the scenes cut together, but there is little sense of intention or human feeling behind it. It raises a question the industry keeps circling: can artificial intelligence generate content that people actually want to watch, not just content that technically qualifies as a film? Seven minutes in, the answer seems to be not yet.

Meanwhile, one of the internet's largest communities for developers has drawn a sharp line. The moderators of Reddit's open source forum, one of the most active communities for software sharing, announced new rules that flat-out ban projects using artificial intelligence. If your project uses artificial intelligence, is made by artificial intelligence, or is built for artificial intelligence, you are not welcome there. The moderators argued that artificial intelligence, as it exists today, is fundamentally incompatible with the principles of the open source movement. It is a culture war moment happening quietly on a message board, but it signals real friction developing inside developer communities around questions of authenticity, attribution, and control.

And separately, a broader structural story is getting renewed attention. Google's artificial intelligence search features have fundamentally disrupted online publishing. When Google's search results now answer questions directly rather than sending users to source articles, the traffic that kept digital news outlets and blogs financially alive simply stops arriving. This is not a new problem, but a detailed look published this weekend draws out just how deep the damage has run across the publishing ecosystem. Outlets that once built business models on search traffic are either pivoting to paid subscriptions, cutting staff, or disappearing entirely. The web's information economy is being reorganized in real time.

First, a signal from health research that is worth understanding clearly. A peer-reviewed meta-analysis published this week in European Neuropsychopharmacology looked at G L P one receptor agonists, the class of drugs that includes widely known weight-loss medications like semaglutide, in patients with schizophrenia spectrum disorders who are being treated with antipsychotics. This is a population that is chronically underserved in metabolic health research. Antipsychotic medications frequently cause significant weight gain and metabolic disruption, which raises cardiovascular risk and shortens lives. The new analysis found that G L P one receptor agonists showed meaningful improvements in metabolic markers in these patients, with a manageable safety profile. What this means in plain language is that a drug class originally developed for diabetes and obesity may have a serious role to play in protecting the physical health of people living with serious mental illness. It does not mean anyone should change medications based on a study. It means researchers are finding that G L P one biology reaches further into human health than the original weight-loss narrative suggested, and that conversations between psychiatrists and metabolic specialists may need to happen a lot more often.

Second, your artificial intelligence move for today. The Apollo economist's report about zero financial payoff for most companies is actually a practical tool you can use right now. Here is what to do. Open whichever large language model you use regularly, whether that is Chat G P T, Claude, Gemini, or another, and give it this specific task. Tell it to audit one process in your work that you believe artificial intelligence could improve. Ask it to identify what measurable outcome you would need to track to know whether artificial intelligence actually helped. Then ask it to design a four-week test. The reason this matters today is that most people and most companies adopt artificial intelligence tools because they feel productive, not because they can prove it. The Apollo finding suggests that feeling and reality are diverging. Running a structured experiment with a clear metric, even a small one, puts you ahead of most organizations that are still hoping the returns will show up on their own. Do not add another tool today. Design one honest test instead.

If you hold positions in that space, the question to ask is whether the companies you own can show real enterprise adoption numbers, not just capability announcements.
