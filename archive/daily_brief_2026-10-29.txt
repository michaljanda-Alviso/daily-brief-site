First, a health research signal worth knowing about. The Department of Health and Human Services moved this week to allow compounding pharmacies to produce certain unapproved peptides under what is called an enforcement discretion policy. In plain language, this means some peptide compounds that were previously in a regulatory gray zone can now be more openly prepared and distributed, at least for now. This is a politically driven shift, tied to the Make America Healthy Again movement and its embrace of peptide wellness protocols. The important distinction for you: regulatory permission to compound something is not the same as clinical evidence that it works. Many of these peptides have limited or no robust human trial data. If you are researching peptides for health or performance, the policy change may affect what is available to you, but it does not change the evidentiary landscape. Talk to a clinician who can separate the credible compounds from the noise.
