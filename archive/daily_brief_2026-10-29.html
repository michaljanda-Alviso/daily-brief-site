Daily Brief — Thursday, October 29, 2026

Big earnings night dominated the wires: Microsoft soared, Alphabet stumbled, and the AI infrastructure trade got complicated. Meanwhile, a major train crash in Spain and a DHS data breach are the stories you need to know before you start your day.

The News

Tech & Deals

  • Microsoft logs 10th consecutive record quarter — $92.3B in revenue, up 19% year over year. Whatever the AI debate, the cash register keeps ringing.
  • Azure revenue: $33.2B, finally disclosed — First time Microsoft put a hard dollar figure on Azure. Still chasing AWS, but closing fast.
  • Microsoft booked $10B from OpenAI in one quarter — Revenue-sharing payments underscore just how financially fused the two companies have become.
  • Upwind raises $400M, spurned Datadog's offer — Cloud security startup valued at $3.8B chose independence over a same-price acquisition. Bold call.
  • Touchscreen MacBook Pro fully specced: $14,549 — Apple's new touchscreen laptop is real — so is the price ceiling. Most buyers will settle for far less.
  • DoorDash buying Instacart for ~$17B — Deal expected to close as early as Thursday morning. Grocery plus delivery, one super-app ambition.

Artificial Intelligence

  • Google launches Gemini 4 Carbon — New frontier model claims top spot on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, beating Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra. Big cybersecurity and agentic improvements highlighted.
  • Alphabet revenue up 25%, but search ad growth slows — AI is reshaping Google's core business. The $127.6B quarter beat revenue targets but spooked investors on margins.
  • Anthropic launches AI spend transparency tools — Ahead of its IPO, Anthropic now lets enterprise customers see exactly where their model budget goes. Smart pre-IPO goodwill move.
  • xAI buying out Mississippi homeowners — if they don't sue — Residents near the Southaven data center are being offered buyouts contingent on dropping litigation. Raises serious ethical flags.
  • Mosseri: AI backlash is "going to get worse" — Instagram's head acknowledged deep, broad public distrust of AI — not just among creatives. Rare candor from inside Big Tech.
  • Microsoft pulling Copilot from smart TVs — LG and Samsung launched it at CES 2025; Microsoft is already winding it down. One of the quieter AI product failures this year.
  • OpenAI & ServiceNow deepen enterprise tie-up — OpenAI models into ServiceNow workflows, and vice versa into ChatGPT and Codex. Enterprise AI stacks keep consolidating.
  • Chrome autofill now reads your Gmail — Gemini with Personal Intelligence can pull form-fill suggestions straight from your inbox. Convenient or creepy — your call.

Markets

  • Chipotle up 7% on same-store sales momentum — Lower-income diners outpaced high-income ones for the first time this year. A consumer health signal worth watching.

For You

Peptides & Health

Big regulatory shift: HHS has moved to allow compounding pharmacies to produce some previously unapproved peptides under an enforcement discretion policy, per STAT News. In plain terms: peptides that were in a grey zone may soon be more widely and legally accessible through compounders.

Separately, Lilly's retatrutide ("triple-G") beat Ozempic head-to-head in a large diabetes trial — lowering blood sugar and cutting weight more effectively. It's investigational, not approved, but this is a meaningful data point if you're tracking the GLP-1/GIP/GLP family. Not medical advice.

AI — Your Move Today

Google's Gemini 4 Carbon just dropped and is already topping benchmarks. Go to Google DeepMind's Carbon page, pick one task you currently use another frontier model for, and run the same prompt in Carbon today. Form your own benchmark — one prompt, one comparison, ten minutes.

Investing — Where to Look

Two directions to watch: Microsoft's blowout quarter and Azure's first disclosed revenue figure ($33.2B) reaffirm enterprise cloud as the durable AI trade. Conversely, the neocloud pullback — CoreWeave and peers fell on Microsoft's capex trim — is a reminder that infrastructure bets are lumpy and sentiment-driven.

Risk sentence: Alphabet's ad growth deceleration is a live signal that AI-native search is beginning to cannibalize legacy ad formats faster than the Street expected — keep that in mind across any digital-ad-exposed position. Not financial advice.
