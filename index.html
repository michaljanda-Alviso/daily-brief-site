Daily Brief — Halloween 2026 🎃

It's October 31 and the tech world has its own horrors to hand out: Intel's worst stock day since the 1970s, Anthropic heading toward a junk credit rating, and the AI spending boom showing its first real cracks. Buckle up.

The News

Tech & Deals

  • Cracks emerge in the AI spending boom — Mounting debt, negative cash flow, and rising investor skepticism are turning the AI infrastructure party into a reckoning.
  • Nvidia may cosign Nscale's West Virginia data center loan — The chipmaker is reportedly willing to backstop a financing deal, a sign that even its biggest customers need help convincing lenders.
  • Salesforce buys defense-tech startup Rune for ~$375M — The AI logistics firm folds into Salesforce's Missionforce platform; the acquisition spree rolls on.
  • Intel posts worst stock day since the 1970s — AI infrastructure demand is cannibalising memory and components, pushing PC prices up and Intel's share price down — wiping $12B off the government's stake.
  • Applebee's left a database password hardcoded in its app — A security researcher found unrestricted access to customer personal data. Classic.
  • Ollie, the privacy-first AI assistant, is shutting down — Another casualty of the crowded AI assistant market — gone in a week.

Artificial Intelligence

  • Google's AI satellite sends its first response from space — Project Suncatcher's TPU-equipped satellite booted up, ran Gemma 4, and beamed a haiku back to Earth. Edge computing just got very high.
  • Apple Intelligence is finally coming to China with iOS 27.3 — Beta lands November 4; full release in December. A major market unlocked after months of regulatory delay.
  • OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Anthropic back unified scraping opt-out standard — Publishers can set a single "policy token" signal instead of blocking each AI firm individually. A meaningful shift for the open web.
  • Pope Leo calls for AI disarmament — "Machines are tools, not persons." The Vatican urges UN states to ban lethal autonomous weapons at next month's Geneva meeting.
  • Altman: RAM belongs in data centers, not on your desk — In a podcast appearance, the OpenAI CEO said personal PC memory is wasteful. Expect the quote to be unpopular with literally everyone who owns a laptop.
  • Altman sympathises with mathematicians — but won't stop the AI math flood — OpenAI's torrent of AI-generated results is overwhelming researchers, and Altman hints there's more in the pipeline.
  • Wikimedia warns a blunt AI copyright ruling could break the open web — The foundation wants courts to allow LLM training on open content while still protecting against chatbot regurgitation.
  • Starbucks wants to serve you a personalised AI menu at the drive-thru — CEO Brian Niccol outlined plans for cameras that recognise loyalty members and show tailored menus before you even roll the window down.

Markets

  • Anthropic faces a high-junk credit rating post-IPO — Blackstone's forecast suggests the AI darling will pay a premium to borrow money, complicating its already eye-watering spend requirements.
  • Silicon Valley asks you to invest in "miracles" — Anthropic's own IPO risk factors range from "existential catastrophe" to "age of miracles." Not your typical prospectus language.
  • S&P revises Meta's outlook to negative — Massive capex and shrinking free cash flow spooked the ratings agency; Meta's AI bet is getting expensive fast.
  • Fed officials signal rate hikes may return — Presidents Musalem and Logan both floated the idea of tightening again — the first concrete hints of a policy pivot back toward higher rates.
  • Big week ahead: Apple earnings, midterms, jobs report — Three market-moving events land in the same five-day window just as investors are already selling tech.
  • Suspicious trading spikes in election prediction markets — CNBC documents how small trades can move low-volume political contracts dramatically — raising integrity questions days before polls close.

World

  • Poland deploys police in Supreme Court stand-off — The government suspended two senior justices and moved to charge them; the constitutional crisis is escalating fast.
  • US approves sale of 500 Patriot missiles to Ukraine — Washington greenlights its only weapon effective against Russian ballistic missiles, a significant escalation in support.
  • G20 'family photo' cancelled over Russia protest — UK Foreign Secretary Miliband confronted Russia's minister over civilian deaths; the group photo was scrapped as a result.
  • Thailand sentences man to 34 years for royal criticism while dressed as Harry Potter — One of 294 prosecutions since 2020 under the country's lèse majesté law; sentences reach 50 years.
  • AI data centers are now a Senate-deciding issue in Maine — Water use, climate concerns, and a heckler at a debate: the battle over data center expansion could flip the chamber.

For You

Peptides & Health

A fresh systematic review in Gynecological Endocrinology finds that GLP-1 receptor agonists significantly improve metabolic markers, reduce androgen levels, and restore menstrual regularity in hyperandrogenic, insulin-resistant women — the population that includes many with PCOS. The authors note the evidence is promising but call for larger randomised trials before widespread clinical adoption.

Plain takeaway: GLP-1 drugs may do more than control weight — they appear to address root hormonal dysfunction in a hard-to-treat group. Watch this space. Not medical advice.

AI — Your Move Today

The new unified AI scraping opt-out standard is live. If you run a website or blog, visit OpenAI's guidance page today and check whether your robots.txt already reflects your training preferences — one file now signals to OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and Anthropic simultaneously. Five minutes, done.

Investing — Where to Look

Two directions worth watching this coming week:

  • Consumer staples rotation — With tech selling off and the AI trade wobbling, defensive consumer names are getting fresh attention. Cramer flagged four names as a starting point for research.
  • Apple earnings on deck — iPhone and services numbers will be the clearest read yet on whether consumer spending is holding. A miss could extend the tech selloff significantly.

Risk: Fed rate-hike signals plus midterm uncertainty make this an unusually volatile week. Size positions accordingly. Not financial advice.
