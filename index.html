Peptides & Health

Nobel Chemistry 2026: mirror-image molecules. This year's Nobel Prize in Chemistry went to Henri B. Kagan and Kenso Soai for cracking the mystery of chiral — or "mirror image" — molecules. In plain language: most biological molecules exist as two mirror versions, but only one version is biologically active. Their work on asymmetric catalysis is why modern drugs can be precisely shaped to fit the body's receptors, reducing side effects dramatically. Every peptide therapeutic you've heard of — from GLP-1 analogues to BPC-157 — depends on this chemistry. Not medical advice.
