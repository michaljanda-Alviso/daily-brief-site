Daily Brief — Saturday, October 17, 2026

A volatile week closes with Meta in damage-control mode, France in flames, and Wall Street hunting for shelter beyond the AI trade. Here is everything that matters this morning, tight and scannable.

The News

Tech & Deals

  • Meta quietly removes scraping language from terms — After publisher backlash, Meta deleted wording that seemed to grant it rights to crawl any linked domain for AI training. The company insists nothing actually changed.
  • TikTok permanently bans Musk documentary ad account — The four-hour Elon Musk documentary is now blocked from paid promotion on TikTok, its second major platform rejection in weeks.
  • Replit makes OpenAI its default, drops Anthropic — CEO Masad confirmed OpenAI replaces Anthropic as the default model provider; Claude stays available but no longer leads the product.
  • Anthropic's workforce is getting more senior — and more meeting-heavy — The AI lab says its staff composition has shifted materially in a year, with fewer junior roles and more coordination time. A sign of scaling pains or growing up?
  • Mark Wahlberg targets the longevity crowd with an affordable gym — Cold plunge, blood panel, protein muffin — no trust fund required, he says. His last gym venture ended in a NYSE delisting.

Artificial Intelligence

  • Websites are slamming the door on AI agents — OpenAI, Meta, and Google promise agents that browse freely on your behalf. Publishers and platforms are increasingly blocking them outright, creating a growing access war.
  • Anthropic's Jack Clark: AI could vastly boost U.S. GDP — Testifying to policymakers, Clark called for international cooperation on "unusual and serious rules" before transformative AI outpaces governance.

Markets

  • AI stocks plunge as inflation ticks up — where the opportunities are — Bank earnings held solid and rotation into non-tech names offered some relief. Analysts say the AI unwind may not be finished.
  • Berkshire backs Bending Spoons, the software consolidator — Less than four months public, Bending Spoons — which buys and revives struggling software brands — has landed a significant vote of confidence from Buffett's firm.
  • South Korea: chip market stays solid at least through 2027 — Despite a global sell-off in chip stocks, Seoul's finance minister says supply tightness keeps the fundamental case intact.
  • BofA names tech stocks to buy ahead of earnings — Several names flagged as having more room to run before their next quarterly prints. Watch this space as reports land next week.
  • Institutional money is piling into U.S. timberland — Pension funds are snapping up vast forest tracts as a decades-long inflation hedge. The catch: it can also be a slow-motion profit trap.
  • Trump waives Russia sanctions Congress just passed — Citing national security, Trump suspended broad new Russia penalties before they took effect. Zelenskyy called it "wrong."

World

  • France: over 1,000 arrested as cost-of-living protests turn violent — More than half a million marched across France; cities saw cars torched, buildings ablaze, and shops looted by nightfall.
  • Anti-government clashes shake Albania's capital Tirana — Riot police and protesters battled in sustained violence throughout Saturday, adding to Europe's weekend of unrest.
  • Israeli settlers attack European diplomats in West Bank — The Israeli military condemned the violence; the PM's office framed it as a "smear" campaign. EU officials called for accountability.
  • At least 17 dead in crush at Hindu festival in Kolkata — A crowd surge at a festival event left many more injured; authorities are investigating crowd management failures.
  • Pope discharged after lung cancer surgery — The pontiff left hospital days after the procedure and is expected to deliver Sunday's Angelus in St Peter's Square.
  • Satellite images reveal luxury yacht near Kim Jong Un's retreat — A 50-metre pool and water slides — in stark contrast to life for most North Koreans.

For You

Peptides & Health

Study: A new real-world analysis published in Nature Medicine examined digital-first delivery of GLP-1 receptor agonist therapy in women. The finding: telehealth-delivered GLP-1 programmes achieved adherence and outcomes comparable to clinic-based care — suggesting the convenience barrier, not the biology, has been the bigger obstacle to access.

Plain-language takeaway: If you or someone you know has been put off GLP-1 treatment by the hassle of in-person visits, digital-first programmes now have real-world data behind them. Worth a conversation with a doctor.

Not medical advice.

AI — Your Move Today

Websites are actively blocking AI agents (as The Verge reports). Your move: audit which research or automation workflows you rely on agents for, then identify one fallback — a direct API, an RSS feed, or a newsletter subscription — for each source that might go dark. Do it before the access war hits your stack.

Investing — Where to Look

Watch 1 — Bending Spoons (software consolidation): Berkshire's early backing of a sub-four-month-old public company is rare. The software roll-up thesis — buy distressed SaaS, cut costs, hold — is worth tracking as rates stay elevated and more software firms struggle.

Watch 2 — Timberland and real assets: With AI stocks volatile and inflation re-accelerating, institutional flows into hard assets like timber are worth noting as a diversification signal. Liquid proxies include timber REITs.

Not financial advice. The AI stock unwind and geopolitical risk around Russia sanctions remain live threats to risk-on positioning.
